01 THE SCIENCE

A FORCE AS OLD AS THE MOON

What is tidal
energy?

Tidal energy captures the movement of seawater as the tides rise, fall, and flow along coastlines.

The Moon’s gravity—and, to a lesser extent, the Sun’s—pulls on Earth’s oceans. As Earth rotates, coastlines experience regular changes in water level and powerful tidal currents.

Underwater turbines work much like wind turbines: moving water turns their blades, which drive a generator to produce electricity. Because tides follow predictable cycles, their timing can be forecast well in advance.

03 A RENEWABLE RHYTHM

A CYCLE THAT KEEPS COMING BACK

Why is it
renewable?

Tides are continuously replenished by the gravitational dance between Earth, Moon, and Sun. We use a small part of a natural cycle that repeats—without burning fuel.

01Naturally replenishedThe tide returns with every cycle.
02Highly predictableTiming can be mapped far ahead.

06 A PRAIRIE PERSPECTIVE

LANDLOCKED, BUT PART OF THE STORY

Tidal energy
in Saskatchewan.

Saskatchewan has no ocean coastline, so ocean tidal power is not geographically suitable here.

That does not mean tidal energy is irrelevant to the province. Canada’s coastal provinces—including Nova Scotia—can explore ocean resources where tides and currents are strong. Electricity from those regions can contribute to a more diverse Canadian energy system, while Saskatchewan builds on resources suited to its own landscape.

09 THE NEXT TIDE

INNOVATION UNDER THE SURFACE

The future is
still unfolding.

Tidal energy is an emerging technology with room to grow. New turbine designs, better materials, and smarter monitoring are helping researchers work toward systems that are more reliable and considerate of marine life.

Canada’s coastlines offer opportunities for research, partnership, and responsible development. Progress depends on listening to coastal communities, understanding ecosystems, and proving that projects can perform over time.
